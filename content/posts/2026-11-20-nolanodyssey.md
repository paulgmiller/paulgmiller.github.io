Finally got to watch Nolan's *Odyssey* after much delay. Kind of funny, because the last two years have been full of *Odyssey* otherwise.

- Read Benjamin *The Odyssey* through [Gillian Cross's children's book](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1536213187).
- Finished Dan Simmons's [*Ilium*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Ilium.html?id=8X6cmAEACAAJ) and [*Olympos*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Olympos.html?id=VSnCKaLwQuEC), which shove the characters of the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* straight into sci-fi.
- That led directly to Tennyson's [*Ulysses*](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/45392/ulysses), which is quite good. "It little profits that an idle king..."
- More amusingly, [The Rest Is History podcast](https://therestishistory.com/series/the-odyssey) covered it in two fun parts. Clearly Odysseus's crew were all Australians.

## Changes I liked

- **Fewer gods.** Totally sensible for realism.
- **Circe.** More grotesque than seductive. A fun reinterpretation.
- **Lotus-Eaters and Calypso combined.** Good compression. The movie was already three hours long.
- **Streamlined cast.** Cutting the Phaeacians, Nestor, and Laertes saves time without losing much.
- **Never seeing Agamemnon's face.** Nice touch.

## Changes I didn't like

- **No "Nobody" trick with the Cyclops.** Classic Odysseus: cleverness undermined by pride.
- **No Aeolus or bag of winds.** A fun adventure lost. Maybe a missed opportunity for some comedy.
- **No dead Achilles.** Especially disappointing given Nolan's emphasis on the costs of war. Achilles's ghost explicitly rejects the glory of dying in battle.
- **Telemachus diminished.** Less capable and independent than in Homer.
- **No Penelope banter or olive-tree bed trick.** We lose the wonderful sense that Penelope is Odysseus's intellectual equal.
- **Sailing west.** After ten years desperately trying to get home, Odysseus immediately leaves again? My wife: "Why would he get on another boat?!"

## Ambivalent

- **Odysseus's PTSD.** Making the Trojan War something he regrets didn't really stick with me, but maybe that says more about me than Homer. The whole "we are the Sea Peoples" angle seemed a bit much.

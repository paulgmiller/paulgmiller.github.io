# Classic Soft Dinner Rolls

**Yield:** 15 rolls  
**Calories:** 139 kcal per roll (as provided)  
**Oven:** 375°F (190°C)  
**Bake:** 15–20 minutes

## Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons sugar
- 1 packet instant yeast
- 1⅓ cups warm milk (100–110°F)
- 2½ cups all-purpose flour, plus more for kneading if needed
- 1 teaspoon salt
- ¼ cup butter, cut into small pieces
- 2 tablespoons melted butter, optional, for topping
- Garlic salt, optional, for garnish

## Instructions

1. Mix the sugar, yeast, and warm milk in a bowl. Let sit for at least 5 minutes; the mixture should foam.
2. Add 2 to 2½ cups of the flour and the salt. Mix until well combined.
3. Add the small pieces of butter and combine with the dough. Add more flour, ¼ cup at a time, until the dough is just slightly sticky to the touch.
4. Knead for 10 minutes.
5. Let the dough rise in a warm place for at least 45 minutes, until nearly doubled in size.
6. Divide into 15 even pieces and roll each into a ball. Arrange evenly in a **greased** casserole dish, then let rise in a warm place for at least another 45 minutes.
7. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C).
8. Bake for 15–20 minutes.
9. If desired, brush with melted butter and finish with garlic salt before serving.

## Notes

- The original recipe lists **2½ cups flour plus more as needed**. Given the amount of milk, be prepared to add substantially more flour to obtain a kneadable dough.
- Editorial cleanup: the dish is greased **before** the shaped rolls are placed in it; the supplied steps listed greasing afterward.

[Original recipe](https://allwaysdelicious.com/classic-soft-dinner-rolls/)

## Thanksgiving critique

**Verdict: A promising soft, buttery roll, but the least predictable dough of the three.** Its mild flavor and buttered top would go with turkey and gravy, and a batch of 15 is manageable. I would skip the optional garlic salt for a traditional Thanksgiving table.

**The milk-to-flour issue:** The stated **1⅓ cups milk is roughly 320 g**, while **2½ cups all-purpose flour is roughly 300 g**. That's **about 105% milk by weight relative to flour**—unusually high for a dough that you're supposed to knead and shape into balls. This isn't an exact baker's hydration percentage, since milk is not pure water, but it's a useful warning sign. The directions *do* say to add extra flour, so the recipe isn't necessarily unusable; it just leaves a large, important quantity unspecified.

**What I'd change:** Expect to need **roughly 3½–4 cups flour total** (about 1–1½ cups beyond the listed 2½), but add it gradually and judge by feel: slightly tacky, not pourable or batter-like. This is an **estimate**, not a tested correction. The uncertain flour requirement makes this a poorer choice for a busy Thanksgiving.

# Whole-Wheat Beer Rolls

**Yield:** 12 rolls  
**Oven:** 400°F (205°C)  
**Bake:** 20–25 minutes

## Ingredients

- 4⅓ cups (520 g) all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
- 1¼ cups (150 g) whole-wheat flour
- 1 tablespoon (9 g) instant dry yeast
- 1½ teaspoons (9 g) kosher salt
- 2 cups (480 mL) beer, such as pale ale
- ¼ cup (85 g) honey
- 4 tablespoons (56 g) unsalted butter
- Neutral oil, for greasing
- Cooking spray, optional
- Egg wash: 1 large egg beaten with 1 tablespoon water, for brushing
- Flaky sea salt, optional

## Instructions

1. In the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook, combine the all-purpose flour, whole-wheat flour, yeast, and kosher salt. Mix for 15–30 seconds.
2. Add the beer, honey, and butter. Mix on low for about 4 minutes, until the dough gathers around the hook. Increase to medium speed and mix for 2 more minutes.
3. Transfer the dough to a lightly greased bowl and cover with plastic wrap. Let rise for about 1 hour, until nearly doubled.
4. Lightly grease a 13 × 9-inch (33 × 23-cm) baking pan with cooking spray or butter. Shape the dough using one of the following methods:
   - **Spirals:** On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough into a rectangle about 15 × 10 inches (35 × 25 cm) and ½ inch (1 cm) thick. Cut into 12 even strips, about 1¼ inches (3 cm) wide. Starting at a short end, roll each strip into a spiral. Arrange spiral-side up, evenly spaced in the prepared pan.
   - **Rounds:** Divide the dough into 12 even pieces (about 100 g each), gently round each into a ball, and place evenly in the prepared pan.
5. Cover the pan with plastic wrap and let rise for 35–45 minutes, until visibly puffy.
6. Toward the end of the rise, heat the oven to 400°F (205°C). Uncover the rolls, brush with egg wash, and sprinkle with flaky sea salt if desired.
7. Bake for 20–25 minutes, until deeply golden brown.

## Thanksgiving critique

**Verdict: The most distinctive option, but not my first pick for the traditional turkey dinner.** The beer, honey, and whole wheat should produce a more savory, malty, slightly nutty roll; the egg wash and optional flaky salt give it a handsome crust. That extra personality could be excellent with cheese, ham, or leftover turkey sandwiches, but it may compete with delicate gravy and other Thanksgiving flavors.

**Dough proportions:** The recipe has **670 g total flour** and **480 g beer**—about **72% beer by weight relative to flour**. That's a much more plausible starting ratio for a kneadable bread dough than the stated ratio in recipe #1. About **22% of the flour is whole wheat**, so expect a heartier bite than an all-white, milk-and-butter roll.

**Practical downside:** It makes only **12 fairly large rolls** (around 100 g each before baking), uses a stand mixer in the instructions, and asks for a hotter **400°F oven**. I'd choose this if the goal were *interesting bread*, rather than the most universally appealing Thanksgiving dinner roll.

# Betty Crocker Dinner Rolls

**Yield:** 24 rolls  
**Oven:** 400°F (205°C), per printed recipe  
**Bake:** 12–18 minutes, per printed recipe  
**Rise:** About 1 hour, then about 30 minutes

## Ingredients

- 3½–3¾ cups Gold Medal all-purpose flour
- ¼ cup sugar
- ¼ cup shortening, margarine, or butter, softened
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 package regular or quick-acting active dry yeast
- ½ cup very warm water (120–130°F)
- ½ cup very warm milk (scalded, then cooled to 120–130°F)
- 1 egg
- Additional softened margarine or butter, for brushing/shaping

## Instructions — Rectangular Pan Rolls

1. In a large bowl, mix 2 cups of the flour with the sugar, shortening (or butter), salt, and yeast.
2. Add the water, milk, and egg. Beat on low speed for 1 minute, scraping the bowl frequently. Beat on medium speed for 1 minute, scraping frequently.
3. Stir in enough remaining flour to make the dough easy to handle.
4. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Knead about 5 minutes, until smooth and elastic.
5. Place in a greased large bowl, turning so the greased side faces up. Cover and let rise in a warm place for about 1 hour, until doubled. The dough is ready when an indentation remains after touching it.
6. Grease a 13 × 9 × 2-inch rectangular pan. Punch down the dough. Roll it into a 13 × 9-inch rectangle on a floured surface, then place it in the pan.
7. Score the dough ¼ inch deep with a sharp knife to mark 24 rolls. Brush with softened margarine or butter. Cover and let rise about 30 minutes, until doubled.
8. Heat oven to 400°F (205°C). Bake 12–18 minutes, until golden brown.

## Variation — Cloverleaf Rolls

1. Grease 24 medium muffin cups (about 2½ × 1¼ inches).
2. After punching down the risen dough, divide it into 72 pieces and shape each piece into a ball.
3. Place 3 balls in each muffin cup. Brush with softened margarine or butter.
4. Cover and let rise about 30 minutes, until doubled.
5. Bake as directed above.

## Variation — Fan-Tan Rolls

1. Grease 24 medium muffin cups (about 2½ × 1¼ inches).
2. After punching down the risen dough, roll half of it into a 13 × 9-inch rectangle on a floured surface. Spread with softened margarine or butter.
3. Cut crosswise into 6 strips, each about 1½ inches wide. Stack the strips evenly; cut the stack into 12 pieces, each about 1 inch wide.
4. Place each piece cut-side down in a muffin cup. Repeat with the remaining dough.
5. Cover and let rise about 30 minutes, until doubled. Bake as directed above.

## Whole-Wheat Variation

Replace **1½–1¾ cups** of the flour added in step 3 with whole-wheat flour.

## Thanksgiving critique

**Verdict: My Thanksgiving winner.** This is the most traditional, crowd-friendly choice: lightly sweet, enriched with egg and milk, and mild enough to carry butter, turkey, and gravy. It also makes **24 rolls**, which is much more useful for a holiday gathering than 12 or 15.

**Dough proportions:** With **3½–3¾ cups flour** (roughly 420–450 g) and **1 cup combined milk and water** (roughly 240 g), it starts with a much more controllable liquid-to-flour ratio than recipe #1. The egg adds additional moisture and richness. As with any cup-measured flour recipe, use the texture—smooth, elastic, and easy to handle—as your final guide.

**What I'd change:** Pick **butter** rather than shortening or margarine for better flavor. Use the **cloverleaf variation** if you want a festive presentation, but the **13 × 9-inch pan version** is the easiest when Thanksgiving prep is hectic. The printed **400°F for 12–18 minutes** is the reliable starting point; the handwritten **375°F / 15 minutes** note may reflect a family adjustment, but I wouldn't rely on it without checking doneness.

**Make-ahead advantage:** Bake the day before, cool completely, store tightly covered, and warm briefly in foil before serving. That avoids needing to manage dough rising while the rest of dinner is coming together.
